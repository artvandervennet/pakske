# How it is put together

```text
app/            pages, components and composables (the Vue side)
  assets/css/   main.css imports Gesso; theme.css is generated; app.css holds the app's own styles
  pages/        index.vue is the home page, lists.vue all your lists, l/[token].vue a list, j/[token].vue a share link, santa.vue and s/[token].vue Secret Santa
  plugins/      gesso.client.ts registers Gesso's custom elements in the browser
i18n/locales/   nl.json and en.json
server/
  api/          the HTTP API; requests about a wish are under lists/[token]/wishes/, share links under invites/
  db/           schema and migrations (Drizzle, SQLite)
  domain/       the rules, as plain functions that take a database; no Nuxt in here
  middleware/   01.security.ts: cache headers, origin check, body size limits
  routes/       uploads/: serves the pictures
  utils/        database handle, HTTP helpers, rate limiter, uploads, the guarded fetch of what people link to, a link's cover, the import from a shop, the cookie of opened lists
shared/
  schemas/      zod schemas for what comes in and what goes out
  utils/        the words on a row and on a notice, the order of the rows, what a tick may do and what an edit sends, the search over lists, shared by the page and the tests
test/           unit tests and the API check
```

- **Accounts**: Firebase Authentication holds the passwords and the Google logins (`server/domain/accounts.ts`, through its REST API in `server/utils/firebase.ts`); `users.firebase_uid` ties an account here to the one there. Firebase is told the account's name as well, for the greeting in its mails. `nuxt-auth-utils` keeps the session in a sealed cookie, with Firebase's refresh token on its server-only side, for what Settings asks of Firebase. Registering asks for a name, an email address and a password of at least 8 characters, and gives the account a first list, "Verlanglijst" or "Wishlist" after the page's language (`createUser` in `server/domain/users.ts`). The cookie holds the account's id and the moment the account was made; a cookie whose account is gone, or whose id now belongs to another account (after a restore, say), logs nobody in, for the API and the page alike (`sessionBelongsTo` in `server/domain/users.ts`). The name and "anoniem geven" in it are read from the database again whenever a page asks who is logged in, so a change on one device reaches the others.
- **Settings**: the name; the email address (`requestEmailChange`, see [Logging in: Firebase](deploying.md#logging-in-firebase)); the password; Google; "Standaard anoniem geven", which makes a new purchase of yours one without your name unless you say otherwise on the wish (`users.anonymous_default`, read by `setPurchase`); light, dark or the system's colours, kept in the browser under `gesso-scheme` as Gesso's own switch does (`app/components/SchemeChoice.vue`; the footer has the same choice as one button, `SchemeButton.vue`, so someone who isn't logged in can choose too); and deleting the account. The address, the password and deleting ask for the current password; an account that only logs in with Google has none, and Firebase then wants a recent login instead.
- **Links in mails** (`app/pages/auth/action.vue`): the page Firebase's mails link to once the project's action URL is `/auth/action` (see [The page behind a mail's link](deploying.md#the-page-behind-a-mails-link)). `checkMailLink` in `server/utils/firebase.ts` reads the code without using it up, and the page says what the link is for. The button sends the code to `followMailLink` in `server/domain/accounts.ts`: a new password is set at Firebase and logs the person in; a new or restored address changes at Firebase and, in the same step, here. The page never follows the `continueUrl` Firebase passes along, so a link can't send anyone to another site.
- **Deleting an account** (`deleteAccount` in `server/domain/accounts.ts`, `deleteUser` in `server/domain/users.ts`): the account's row is deleted, and the database deletes what points at it: their purchases on other people's wishes, the lists they opened, their share links, their notices, the links that could still undo one of their changes, and their place among the owners of lists and wishes. A list they owned alone is deleted first, the way they would have deleted it themselves (`removeList` in `server/domain/lists.ts`): whoever was getting something from it keeps seeing that for thirty days, and then the purge erases it (see Erasing below). A list they share is the other owners' from then on, with its wishes. Their notes on other people's wishes stay: `notes.user_id` becomes null and the note reads "Someone else". What they wrote to the owners of a list stays as well, as unnamed as it was, and so do their answers on a list they shared, without their name. Both go when that wish or list is erased. Nothing keeps the name or the address, so an account made later with the same address starts with a first list of its own and nothing of the old one. A picture only a link of theirs still remembered is removed. Before the row goes, the account is taken out of its Secret Santa groups (`leaveEveryGroup`, see Secret Santa below).
- **A wish on the page** (`app/components/WishRow.vue`): a row that unfolds in place into a card as wide as the list. Nothing is pushed in: the tick's column is as wide as one box on every row, whatever the wishes ask for. A wish asked for once has a box; one asked for more than once, or without a limit, has a count the size of that box, which shows your number and gives one more when pressed. Lowering is done in the opened card, which has the full stepper ("I'm giving 2 of 3"). The box, the count and the card's control share one state per row (`app/composables/useWishTick.ts`), and what they may do is in `shared/utils/wish-tick.ts`. For a buyer, a wish they give has an edge in the accent beside its tick and is never greyed (`givenByMe`); the line under the title names who else gives, not themselves (`wishLine` in `shared/utils/wish-line.ts`); a wish others took fully is greyed and crossed out (`isCrossedOut`). An owner's own ticks look as they did.
- **The opened wish**: its head has the title and small icon buttons (`app/components/AppIcon.vue`): "Share" for an owner on their own list, which opens the share dialog for that one wish; "Add to my list" for a buyer; and the one that folds it. An owner's title can be 200 characters (`WISH_TITLE_MAX`); the field takes more than that, says so while it is too long, pointing to the description, and saves nothing until it fits. An owner's card has the photo, the description with "How many", and the links, in three columns where the card is wide enough, each part saving by itself (`WishEditor.vue`); under them what they have of it themselves and who they share it with. On a list you own the card ends in a quiet "Delete", which asks first; the list's edit mode deletes several wishes at once. Everyone else gets the photo, the text, the links, and one block to give the wish: a wide button that stays pressed, or the stepper, with "Give anonymously" under it, then who gives, then the notes (`WishBuyerDetails.vue`). A visitor who isn't logged in gets the text, the links and a way to log in. A wish's picture opens large in the page's one viewer (`PictureViewer.vue`, reached through `usePictureViewer`): a press on the picture for everyone who doesn't own the wish, and "Vergroten" under the tile for an owner, whose tile itself chooses another photo. The viewer is a `<dialog>` as large as the picture: as big as the screen allows and at most twice the picture's own size. A press on the picture, or "Inzoomen", makes it three times as large in a frame that scrolls, which is what makes it larger on a phone; a finger or a mouse drags it around. It closes with "Sluiten", Escape, or a press beside the picture.
- **Giving anonymously**: a tick sends "anonymous" only when the person used that switch on the wish itself (`tickBody` in `shared/utils/wish-tick.ts`). A plain tick leaves it out, and the server keeps a purchase as it is and gives a new one the account's "Standaard anoniem geven", or no name at all when the buyer drew one of the wish's owners in a Secret Santa (`drewAnOwnerOf`); the list's page then says so and starts the switch on (`drewOwners` beside the list's view). So the setting counts from the moment it is changed, also in a tab that was opened before.
- **Lists**: a list has a title, one or more owners (`list_owners`) and a token of 16 random bytes in base64url, made by `newListToken` in `server/domain/tokens.ts`. The token is the list's address, `/l/<token>`, and the only way in: no page lists other people's lists. Owners rename a list (the address stays) and can give it a note (`lists.note`, at most 1000 characters): under the "Wishes" title, beside the wishes on a wide screen and above them on a narrow one, for everyone who opens the list, logged in or not, and for an owner a field there that saves by itself, like a wish's description. A deleted list and an address the list no longer has come without it, and a link's preview leaves it out. A list with one owner can be deleted; with more, each owner can leave it and the last one deletes it. Both are in the edit mode of the list, which "Edit" in its title band starts.
- **Sharing a list**: "Delen" in a list's title band, for everyone who can open the list, opens one sheet (`ShareSheet.vue`, with the list's part in `ListShare.vue`): a modal `<dialog>` that is a centred card on a wide screen and lies against the bottom edge on a narrow one. For an owner it starts with the friends the list hasn't gone to yet, as a row of pictures with names that scrolls sideways and snaps; one press on a friend shares the list with them (`PUT /api/lists/[token]/friends/[id]`), their picture gets a tick and they leave the row. Under it "Link kopiëren", and "Meer apps", which opens the device's own share sheet where the browser has one (both are Gesso's `<art-share>`). The message for the share sheet says whose list it is ("my" from its owner, "our" from one of several, "the" from anyone else) and asks the receiver to make an account, since only someone logged in sees what was ticked. Nothing is taken back in the sheet: which lists a friend finds is changed on the friends page. Inviting a co-owner is kept to the list's edit mode: "Mede-eigenaar uitnodigen" there opens the same sheet with the list's invitation link, its one sentence, and the friends who weren't invited in the last 30 days. The friend link on the friends page ("Vriend toevoegen") goes out through the same sheet, and nothing opens the device's share sheet by itself. A link that was just made for wishes is shown with a share button and words of its own (`OneTimeLink.vue`, `ShareButton.vue`).
- **The preview of a link** (`app/composables/useSiteHead.ts`, `usePreview.ts`): the picture, title and sentence a chat app shows with a link. Every page carries the site's own. A list's page names the list and its owners, and the login page says what kind of link someone was sent ([Who sees what](who-sees-what.md)). The picture is `public/preview.png`; `scripts/preview-image.html` is the page it is a screenshot of, and says how to make it again.
- **A new link**: "Nieuwe link" in a list's edit mode gives the list another address (`relinkList` in `server/domain/lists.ts`). The list, its wishes, its owners and its open share links stay; the address it had goes to `list_old_tokens` and stops opening the list. Everyone who doesn't own the list loses it from "From others", which would otherwise hand them the new address, and the other owners get a notice. An old address keeps one use, so nobody is left with a tick they can't take back: someone logged in who was giving something from the list gets a bare page with only those wishes and their own tick, to lower or withdraw (`oldLinkView` in `server/domain/list-view.ts`; `setPurchase` and `removePurchase` are the only other code that resolves an old address). An owner who opens an old address is sent on to the new one; everyone else gets "not found".
- **Placements**: `list_wishes` says which lists show a wish. Taking a wish off a list sets `removed_at` on its placement and keeps the row; `cleared_at` beside it says the owner chose that whoever bought it shouldn't keep seeing it there (see [who sees what](who-sees-what.md)), and a wish that comes back to the list loses both. One rule in `server/domain/placement.ts` covers every way a wish leaves a list (deleting a wish or a list, unlinking, undo): `takeOffList`, which ends with `settleOwners`. `settleOwners` is the only code that writes `wish_owners`: it makes a wish's owners the owners of the live lists that show it, and marks a wish deleted when no live list shows it any more. A list whose only owner deleted their account still shows its wishes, so those stay without an owner. Leaving a list calls `settleOwners` directly. Each placement has a join order that only goes up, so the site knows who had a wish first. Each placement also has a position: a list shows its wishes by position, the same for everyone, and only its owners change it. A wish that arrives or comes back goes to the bottom; the copy an unlink leaves takes the original's place.
- **Together and Copy**: on a wish on someone else's list, "Add to my list" (a button in the opened wish's head) asks which of your lists it goes on (the one used last is chosen) and offers two buttons (`server/domain/sharing.ts`). Together ("Samen"; `linked` in the code) puts the same wish on your list: you own it at once, stop seeing who buys it, and its earlier owners get a notice with Undo. Copy makes a new wish of your own with the same title, text, links, picture and amount; nobody is told. Either can make a new list on the way. Every copy is made by `copyWishOnto`, also the ones a share link gives and the one an unlink leaves.
- **Unlink**: an owner can unlink anyone who became an owner after them; someone who joined later can only take the wish off their own list. Owners of one list can't unlink each other. The wish doesn't change. The unlinked person gets, on each of their lists that showed it, a plain copy in the same place, without purchases, and a notice. Purchases stay on the original, theirs included.
- **What a link remembers**: when a linker changes a field (title, text, links, amount, picture), their link remembers what it held before their first change, in `link_changes` (`recordChanges` in `server/domain/undo.ts`). When anyone else changes that field later, every other link forgets it, so undo never takes back a newer change. Undo puts the remembered fields back, unlinks the linker, and removes the link and every notice about it. When the last notice about a link is dismissed, the link and what it remembered are deleted; from then on the changes are simply the wish.
- **Friends** (`server/domain/friends.ts`, `/friends`): two people who chose each other once. There is no search for people. Someone is asked from a list of theirs that the asker can open (the owner's name in the line "van …" under the list's title is a button that opens a small card with "Vrienden worden", `askFriend`), or accepts a friend link, `/f/<token>`, which works once and for 30 days (`makeFriendLink`) and, like every link that is handed on, goes out through the device's share sheet with a message of its own. A request names its sender and the list it came from, and waits on the receiver's home page and friends page. Declining tells nobody: the request stays as `declined`, and to its sender it looks and behaves like a waiting one, so it can't be noticed or sent again. Two people who ask each other are friends at once. A person can have 100 friends and 20 requests out.
- **Lists shown to friends**: being friends shows nothing. An owner switches a list on for a friend, on the friends page or in the list's share sheet (`list_friends`: list, friend, and the owner who shows it, so on a joint list each owner's choices are their own). The friend then has the list under "Van anderen" ("From others") on the Lists page and the home page, and gets a notice. It is no key: the list opens for anyone who has its link, which the friends page says where its switches are. Other people's lists are one row there, whichever way they came: `listOthersLists` in `server/domain/visits.ts` puts the lists friends show first and the ones opened by a link after them, and a list is never in it twice. To the owner a friend has a list when it was switched on for them or when they opened its link themselves: both show as on, on the friends page and in the share sheet, and switching it off forgets the friend's visit too, so the list leaves their page however it got there. Opening a list while a friend shows it is not remembered as a visit, so it is gone from the page when the friend switches it off; a list of a friend's that was opened by its link stays a visited one on the opener's side, and reads as "has it" on its owner's. "Weghalen" takes a list off the page whichever way it got there (`takeOffPage`): the visit is forgotten and a shown list is hidden for that person alone (`hidden_at`); the owner isn't told. Switches go with the friendship, with the owner leaving the list, with the list, and with either account. A list that gets a new link stays shown, at its new address.
- **Share links**: an owner ticks wishes in the list's edit mode and presses "Share", or presses "Share" in an opened wish for that one wish, or presses "Mede-eigenaar uitnodigen" in the list's edit mode, and gets a link `/j/<token>` (`server/domain/invites.ts`). Its token is made like a list token and is never the list's. The link needs a login. For wishes, the owner first chooses how to share. Copy, the default: everyone who opens the link gets copies of their own, once per account, for 30 days; the originals stay their owners' alone and nobody is told. Together: whoever opens it becomes an owner of the wishes, the maker gets a notice, and the link works once. Either way the person who opens it sees the wishes with a box each, unticks the ones they don't want, and chooses one of their lists (or a new one). A wish they own already can't be shared with them again, but can be copied. A link for the list is an invitation (`listInviteLink`): for 7 days it makes everyone who opens it a co-owner of the whole list, so it can go into a group chat, and every earlier owner is told of each person who joins. Each owner has one per list; asking for it again gives the same address and 7 days from then. It ends when the list gets a new link, and goes with its maker when they stop owning the list. A link closes when its list is deleted, when its maker no longer owns the list, when none of its wishes is on the list any more, and, if it works once, when it is used. A link holds at most 50 wishes; a list has at most 20 open links (unused ones, and copy links that haven't expired), and making one more drops the oldest. What a link gives can also be sent to a friend (`sendInvite`, the same route with `friends`): the dialog that shares wishes offers the person's friends who don't own the list already, and with nobody chosen it makes the link as before; for the list itself a friend is invited with one press on their picture in the sheet that edit mode opens. An invitation to a list lasts 30 days and isn't taken back. When the friend closes its notice the send stays, marked (`invites.declined_at`), and gives them nothing: the sheet leaves invited friends out, and it must leave out the same friends whether they said no or said nothing, until the 30 days are over for both. Each friend chosen gets a send of their own (`invites.sent_to`) and a notice that leads to the same page a link opens; nothing changes until they say yes there. A send has no address to pass on: its token is never given to its sender and matches nothing for anyone but that friend. It is deleted once it is answered, when the two stop being friends, and when it is sent again; wishes that were sent are also deleted when the friend closes the notice (their "no", which nobody is told); its notice goes with it, and is not shown once the send can no longer be taken. Sends are not among a list's 20 open links; one person can have 20 waiting for one friend.
- **Secret Santa** (`server/domain/santa.ts`, `/santa` and `/s/<token>`): something extra beside the lists, which it changes nothing about. A group (`santa_groups`) has a name and a token made like a list's. The token is its address and its invitation: whoever opens `/s/<token>` while logged in gets the group's name, who runs it and how many are in, and can join, also after the names are drawn. A member (`santa_members`) puts in one of their own lists, or none, and can change that at any time. Every other member is shown that list without a link being sent: its name is a link beside the member on the group's page, and it is among their lists "Van anderen" (`listGroupLists`, which `listOthersLists` in `server/domain/visits.ts` puts after the lists friends show and before the ones opened by a link), with the group's name on its row. It is no key and no visit: the list opens for anyone who has its link, and opening it while the group shows it is not remembered, so it is off the page again when either leaves the group, when the member puts in another list, and when the group ends. Such a row has no "Weghalen". What someone ticks there counts for "Wat je geeft" like a list they opened. Whoever joined first runs the group (`membersOf` orders by when people joined): they rename it, take someone out, draw the names and end the group. When that person leaves, or their account goes, whoever joined next runs it, so a group never waits on an account that is gone. A group holds 50 people and a person can be in 20 groups. "Namen trekken" (`drawNames`) needs three members: `drawOrder` shuffles the members with `crypto.randomInt` and starts over whenever anyone would draw themselves, so every draw in which nobody has their own name is as likely as any other. With exactly three, each member can work out who drew them, and the question before the draw says so. Someone who joins after the draw has no name and is nobody's name, so the draw is as it was. Anyone can leave, and whoever runs the group can take anyone out, at any time. When that person had a name, the draw no longer counts for anyone (`takeOut`): every name is wiped, not only the two that the leaving touches, or the page would show who had drawn whom. The question before it says what it costs: the names have to be drawn again, whoever bought something did so for the name they had then, and everyone is told. The group's page then reads the same for everyone who stays (`lapsed`), and a draw that no longer counts is joined like a group that never drew. While any member has no name (a latecomer, everyone after someone with a name went, or the one person a deleted account can leave with nobody), the button reads "Opnieuw trekken" and the same `drawNames` makes a new draw of everyone. It doesn't fit a newcomer into the old one, which would tell the member whose name changed who the newcomer has. While everyone has a name the names can't be drawn again, or whoever runs the group could draw until they liked their own. Each member's page shows the name they drew behind a button, with a link to the list that person put in, while it is still theirs. Who drew whom is in `santa_members.gives_to` and is read for the member it belongs to and for nobody else (see [who sees what](who-sees-what.md)). An account that is deleted hands the name it drew to whoever drew it (`leaveEveryGroup`), so everyone left still gives to someone; only when those two had drawn each other is one person left with nobody. Joining, being taken out and the draw each leave a notice, which goes with the group: `santa_joined` for whoever runs the group, or `santa_joined_late` after the draw, which says the names must be drawn again; `santa_removed` for someone who was taken out; `santa_lapsed`, in the name of whoever went, for everyone who stays when a draw stops counting; and `santa_drawn` or `santa_redrawn` for every other member. The header has a link for someone logged in; the home page shows the person's groups only when there are any.
- **Writing to the owners** (`server/domain/messages.ts`, `ListMessages.vue`, `MessageThread.vue`): under a list's wishes is a band where someone who opened the list writes to its owners ("Een bericht voor Art"), to ask or tell them something. The owners read it as from "Iemand": the server never sends them who wrote. Each person has one conversation per list (`list_threads`, `list_messages`); an owner sees all of them in the same band ("Berichten", there once someone wrote) and answers with their own name. `POST /api/lists/[token]/messages` writes: from someone who doesn't own the list into their own conversation, from an owner into the conversation it names. The conversations come with the list (`threads`, added by the list's route like the owners to befriend). Each side gets one notice per list, replaced by the next message; the owners' names nobody and shows a speech balloon where a picture would be. Both notices lead to `#messages` on the list. A message can be deleted by whoever wrote it (`DELETE /api/messages/[id]`), a whole conversation by an owner, who is asked first (`DELETE /api/lists/[token]/threads/[id]`). A message is 1000 characters at most, a conversation 200 messages, a list 200 conversations, and one account sends 30 messages in fifteen minutes. Someone who isn't logged in is asked to log in, and comes back to the band. [Who sees what](who-sees-what.md) has what keeps the writer unnamed, and its limits.
- **Profile pictures**: a person can put up a picture in Settings (`ProfilePicture.vue`, `PUT` and `DELETE /api/me/image`, `users.image_file`); the browser makes it small first, as it does a wish's photo, and the file lives under `uploads/` with those. Without one, `PersonPicture.vue` draws a round study in two of Gesso's paints from the account's id, the way a wish without a photo gets one. Only friends are sent someone's picture: it is in what the friends page and a list's friends answer, in a Secret Santa group for the members who are the viewer's friends, and in nothing about a list itself. It goes with the account.
- **Removing a co-owner** (`removeOwner` in `server/domain/lists.ts`): in a list's edit mode an owner has a quiet button per co-owner who joined the list after them (`coOwners` beside the list's view, for owners only). Seniority decides: the first owner can remove everyone, and nobody can remove someone who was there before them. For the person removed it is what leaving is (`dropOwner`): they keep only the wishes that are also on another list of theirs, and what they showed or sent friends of this list goes. They get a notice; the other owners don't. Every invitation link of the list ends with it, or the one still going round would let them back in.
- **Joint lists**: a co-owner owns the list and every wish on it, those already there and those added later, and finds it under "Your lists", which names the other owners. Leaving takes away the list and every wish on it that isn't also on another list of yours (`leaveList` in `server/domain/lists.ts`). The last owner can't leave (`last_owner`); a list with more than one owner can't be deleted (`list_shared`).
- **Notices** (`server/domain/notices.ts`): someone linked a wish of yours (with Undo), you were unlinked from a wish, someone used your link and now shares wishes with you, someone became a co-owner of your list, a friend sent you something, and so on. They are behind a bell in the header (`AppHeader.vue`), with a dot on it while some are new. The bell is one of the header's actions, which `<art-header>` keeps in the bar at every width. What folds away under "Menu" on a small screen is the links and "Uitloggen": a button after the bell that carries `fold`, which Gesso 1.8's header moves into the menu there. The menu closes on a click outside the header, on Escape, and when the page under it changes. Pressing it asks for `GET /api/notices` (the notices and the friend requests that wait) and opens `NoticesHost.vue`: a panel that hangs under the bell on a wide screen (Gesso's anchored `.menu` popover), a sheet against the bottom edge on a narrow one. Opening it counts as having seen them. A row (`NoticeList.vue`) is the sender's picture (their own only when they are a friend), the sentence and when it happened (`ago` in `shared/utils/ago.ts`). Only a real choice is a button: yes or no to a friend request, "Bekijken" on something a friend sent, and Undo; a notice that only points somewhere has its sentence as the link, and a cross wipes it. "Alles wissen" at the top wipes every notice that only says something, in one request (`POST /api/notices/dismiss`, `dismissNotices` in `server/domain/undo.ts`). A row with a choice in it stays: a friend request, something a friend sent, and a notice with Undo. The route never touches a notice about something a friend sent, whoever names it, because closing that one is the answer no. The sheet's own close button is the word "Sluiten", so no cross stands above the rows' crosses. Logged out there is no bell, and on a small screen no "Menu" either: the header has `nofold`, so its one link, "Lijsten", stays in the bar beside "Inloggen".
- **Remembered lists**: opening someone else's list puts it under "From others" ("Van anderen"). Logged in, that is a row in `list_visits`. Logged out, its token goes into the `opened-lists` cookie (at most 50). A cookie and not local storage, because the server builds the pages. Logging in or registering moves the cookie's lists into the account and clears the cookie. It is the only cookie a logged-out visitor gets: the login cookie starts at the login, so `sessionUser` in `server/utils/http.ts` doesn't read the session of a request that brings no login cookie (reading it would set one). "Remove" only takes a list off your visited lists; the link keeps working. Opening a list of your own writes the same row, and only orders your own lists: the one you opened or joined last comes first (`listOwnedLists` in `server/domain/lists.ts`). It is never shown as visited while you own it; a list you left is under "From others" only if you opened it yourself or a friend shows it to you. Nobody sees who opened their list.
- **The home page and the lists page**: the home page shows the five most recent lists of your own and the five most recent of other people's (`GET /api/home`), each with a button under it to the rest ("Alle lijsten bekijken", which names how many there are when the page leaves some out). Under them, for someone who ticked anything, is "Wat je geeft" ("What you’re giving"): every wish they give, under the name of who it is for, the people they ticked something for last first (`listGiven`, in the same answer). A row is the wish's picture and name, the list it is on, and how many when the wish asks for more than one; it opens that wish on its list (`/l/<token>#wish-<id>`), where the tick can be changed. [Who sees what](who-sees-what.md) says what is left out. `/lists` has all of them (`GET /api/lists`) as two tabs, "My lists" and "From others", and one search field that filters both in the browser, on a list's title and its owners' names, whatever the capitals and accents (`shared/utils/list-search.ts`). The tab and the search are in the address (`?tab=visited&q=…`). Logged out it shows only the lists this browser opened. "Edit" in its title band starts an edit mode (`app/composables/useListsEdit.ts`): every row gets a box, and the bar above the lists acts on the ticked lists of the tab in view. Your own are deleted, after a question that names them; one you share with someone is left instead, as on its own page. Other people's are taken off the page without a question, as "Remove" on a row does. It sends one request per list, the same ones a single list uses, and stops at the first that fails. Neither page can show a list you don't own and never opened.
- **Amounts**: a wish has a whole number or no limit (`quantity` is `NULL`). Adding one takes only a title; the amount starts at one. Each person has one purchase per wish with their own number, whichever list they bought it through. `setPurchase` in `server/domain/purchases.ts` checks the total in one synchronous transaction, so two buyers can't both take the last one.
- **Images**: the browser scales a picture to at most 1600 pixels and re-encodes it as JPEG before uploading (`app/composables/useImageResize.ts`). The server checks the size (3 MB) and the first bytes of the file, and stores it under a random name. A copy shares its picture's file with the original, and a link keeps a replaced picture while it may still put it back. A file is removed only when no wish, deleted or not, and no link uses it any more (`unusedImages` in `server/domain/undo.ts`). Pictures are served with `Cache-Control: private`: a browser may keep one for good, but a proxy or a tunnel's edge may not, or it would go on handing out a picture that was deleted here.
- **Erasing** (`server/domain/purge.ts`, run once a day by `server/plugins/03.cleanup.ts`): deleting a list or a wish only marks it, because its buyers keep seeing it for thirty days (`KEPT_FOR_BUYERS_DAYS`). After those days `purge` takes the row out of the database, and the database deletes what hangs on it: placements, purchases, notes, conversations, visits, links and notices. A share link or a friend link goes thirty days after it ran out or was used. A live list nobody owns, left by an account deleted before accounts took their own lists along, is deleted the same way a list is. Then `sweepUploads` removes the picture files no row points at (`picturesInUse`), which also picks up a file a failed removal left behind. The same plugin drops the rate limiter's counters once their window is over, every minute, since their keys hold an IP address or a typed email address.
- **A copy of your data** (`exportAccount` in `server/domain/export.ts`, `GET /api/me/export`, "Download your data" in Settings): a JSON file with what the site keeps about the person. It leaves out what the pages keep from them: who gives their wishes, who wrote to their lists, whether a request or an invitation they sent was declined, and other people's email addresses. A new table or column that holds something about a person belongs in it.
- **Links and their cover**: a wish has up to five links, in the owner's order, and a change sends the whole list (`urls`). While a wish has no photo of its owner's, the server fetches the page of the first link and takes the picture that page names as its cover (`og:image`, `twitter:image` or `image_src`), so a shop sees the server's address and never a visitor's (`server/utils/link-cover.ts`). An uploaded photo always wins; removing it brings the cover back, and changing or removing the first link replaces or removes the cover. The fetch itself is in `server/utils/guarded-fetch.ts`, which an import uses too. It goes to public addresses only, over http or https on ports 80 and 443: it looks the name up first, refuses when any of its addresses is this machine or a private network, connects to the address it checked, and checks each of at most three redirects the same way. It gives up after 5 seconds, reads at most 512 KB of the page and 3 MB of picture, sends no cookies, and fetches at most 30 covers per account in 10 minutes. A cover that can't be fetched is left out without a message.
- **Importing from a shop** (`server/utils/shop-import.ts`, `app/components/ImportDialog.vue`): "Importeren uit een winkel" under an owner's list opens a sheet with one field to paste links into. `POST /api/import/read` reads them (`readImport`) and answers with what it found; nothing is on a list yet. A link to a wishlist someone shares at bol.com or Amazon gives every wish on it. bol.com's page carries its first 24 in the data for its own scripts (`bolList`); for the rest the server sends bol.com's API the request that page sends when it is scrolled (`bolMoreRequest`), which names the query by an id that bol.com changes whenever it changes the query. Amazon's page holds ten wishes and names the next page until the list ends (`amazonList`). Any other link is taken for the page of one product and gives one wish with the product's name: from the page's JSON-LD, else its `og:title`, else its `<title>` (`pageTitle`). The sheet shows the wishes with a box each, all ticked except the ones whose link the list has already (`alreadyListed` in `shared/utils/wish-import.ts`), and `POST /api/lists/[token]/wishes/import` puts the ticked ones on the list, all of them or none (`addWishes`). Each becomes a wish with that one link and an amount of one. Their pictures come after that, one at a time across the whole site (`coverImported`): the picture the shop's list showed (a small one at bol.com, a larger one at Amazon), and for a pasted product link the cover of its page. The request waits 6 seconds for them, and the rest show when the list is next opened. Every request to a shop goes out as a cover's does, under the site's own name and without cookies. A shop that turns that away is not asked in any other way: its links come back as unread, and a list bol.com's API keeps part of comes back with the wishes its page held and the count of the rest. bol.com answers for the page of a shared wishlist and turns the server away from the pages of its products, so a pasted link to a bol.com product is unread, and a wish with such a link gets no cover. One paste is read for at most 100 links and 100 wishes, within about 25 seconds and 2 MB a page. An account reads 5 pastes and adds wishes 10 times in 10 minutes.
- **Limits**: 200 lists ever and 50 at once per person; 500 wishes ever and 200 at once per list. They also count lists made by "Add to my list" and share links, and wishes put on a list by linking, copying or a link. The copy an unlink leaves is not counted, so unlinking never fails.
- **The privacy page and the terms** (`/privacy`, `/terms`): open to everyone, linked from the footer and from the register form. Each is an outline of sentences from the locale files (`shared/utils/legal.ts`), drawn by `app/components/LegalPage.vue`. Who runs the site comes from `NUXT_PUBLIC_OPERATOR_NAME`, `_EMAIL`, `_NETWORK` and `_BACKUPS`; a sentence that names them is only in the outline when the setting is filled in. `test/legal.test.ts` checks that every sentence the outlines name exists in both languages and that none is left over. A change to what the site keeps, to a cookie, or to who gets to see something belongs on the privacy page too, with a new date in `privacy.updated`.
- **The about page** (`/about`): what the site is and what it does, open to everyone and linked from the footer only, not the header. A band per subject in Gesso's paints, built from an outline of sentences like the privacy page (`shared/utils/about.ts`, checked by the same test). A feature that changes what someone can do belongs there too.
- **Languages**: Dutch at `/…`, English at `/en/…`. The server answers with error codes; the words are in the locale files under `errors` and `fields`.
- **Guard rails**: five wrong passwords lock an account for fifteen minutes, registration is limited per address, and requests that change something are refused when they come from another site. A tab that comes back into view checks who is logged in and reloads if that changed (`app/plugins/session-watch.client.ts`).

## Gesso

Gesso is installed from git, pinned to a tag in `package.json`. The theme "Ribbon" is this project's own: three pigments in `theme.json`, built by `pnpm theme`.

Things to know when changing the UI:

- The components need the DOM, so they are only imported in `app/plugins/gesso.client.ts`. The server sends plain HTML and the elements upgrade it. `app.css` styles buttons before they upgrade, so the page doesn't jump.
- Every page starts with a band in the deep paint (`PageHead.vue`, or the hero on the home page). `<art-header>` takes its paint from the first band.
- `<art-header>` reads its children and attributes once. To change it, change the element's `key`. It also copies each link's text into its scroll repaint, which is why the mark for new notices is an empty element with an `aria-label` and not hidden text.
- Pass boolean attributes as `value || undefined`. Vue renders `false` as `wet="false"`, which Gesso reads as present.
- `<art-signature>` writes its text into `innerHTML`. It only gets fixed text.
- The header's "Menu" comes from Gesso and is English in both languages.

`app.css` marks the blocks that fill gaps in Gesso as candidates to move there: among them the rows of a list, the rows that unfold into a card, the row that is yours among rows that are not, the tick box with its count and stepper, the icons with the buttons made of them, the switch, the + row that turns into the new row, the small picture with its painted study, text edited where it is read (`.inline-edit`, used for an owner's wish and a list's title), the amount with its stepper and switch, the photo tile that is its own control, the choice of a list with every list on view, the bar for choosing wishes in edit mode, the single-use link, and the mark in the header.

## Not included

- Email of the site's own: Firebase mails only a forgotten password and the link for a new address. Notices stay inside the site, and the address someone registers with isn't confirmed.
- Fetching a price from a shop link, or a title for a link someone adds by hand. An import reads titles; a link added to a wish gets only its picture, and only when it is the first.
- Importing from a shop that answers only a browser (LEGO, Zalando, Dreamland and Decathlon did in November 2026), or a shared list whose page the shop's robots.txt closes (Coolblue, IKEA). Their products can be pasted as links where the shop answers at all.
- Prices, restoring a deleted wish.
- Seeing who opened your list (except that a friend who did counts as having it), or taking a list away from someone who has the link.
- Withdrawing a share link. One keeps working until it is used, expires or closes; making 20 newer ones on the same list drops it.
- Telling anyone when someone leaves a list.
- In a Secret Santa: drawing the names again while everyone has one, keeping two people from drawing each other, a budget or a date, and a member taking a finished group off their own page. Whoever runs the group ends it.

Known consequences of joint wishes, accepted in the design:

- Anyone who is logged in and can open your list can link one of your wishes and then, as an owner, change it. The notice's Undo puts their changes back and unlinks them.
- Once you no longer own a joint wish, you are a buyer of it again wherever you can open a list that shows it, so you then see who bought it. It is no longer your wish.
